About Stackly

Built by people who've stood on the floor.

Stackly MRP started with a simple complaint from a plant manager: "the schedule in the system and the schedule on the floor are two different things." We've spent six years closing that gap.

Our mission

Close the gap between the plan and the plant.

Manufacturing software has historically forced a choice: powerful planning tools that ignore floor reality, or floor-friendly apps with no real planning logic underneath. Stackly refuses that trade-off. We build finite-capacity planning that updates from real machine and labor signals, and floor tools simple enough that an operator opens them without training.

Every module ships from the same conversations: sitting in on a shift change, watching a scheduler fight a spreadsheet, tracing a non-conformance back through three systems that don't talk to each other. We build the tool we watched people need.

What we hold to

Four things we don't compromise on.

Real-time, not nightly

If a number is more than a few seconds old, it's a report — not a system of record. We build for live state.

Traceable by default

Genealogy isn't a premium add-on. Every lot, every operation, every shipment — tracked from day one.

Operator-first UI

If it needs a training manual on the shop floor, we redesign it. Two taps, not twelve.

Open by design

Documented APIs and EDI out of the box. Your data leaves whenever you want it to.

How we got here

Six years, four modules, one loop.

2020

Founded on a shop floor

Started as a scheduling tool for a single contract manufacturer in Coimbatore, built alongside the planners who'd use it.

2022

Procurement & inventory modules ship

Auto-generated POs and live inventory ledgers closed the loop between what's planned and what's on the shelf.

2024

Traceability & quality holds

Full genealogy tracking launched after three customers asked for it in the same audit cycle.

2026

640+ plants, one live system

Stackly now schedules production across discrete, process and mixed-mode manufacturers on five continents.
